Editorial Methods

How this fact-check works — and what it doesn't claim to do.

What we evaluate

Every claim on this page is a specific, verifiable statement made by a candidate in a speech, interview, debate, or official communication. We only publish evaluations for claims that are fact-checkable — meaning they assert a concrete, checkable fact, not an opinion, a prediction, or a policy preference.

Claims are linked to the original source where possible. We do not evaluate intent, character, or values.

Verdict definitions

Supported

The claim's core assertion is accurate and supported by primary sources. Minor omissions or rounding that do not materially change the meaning are noted but do not change the verdict.

Mixed / misleading

The claim contains a verifiable kernel of fact but is framed, scoped, or contextualized in a way that creates a materially misleading impression. Common causes: cherry-picked timeframes, omitted denominators, projection presented as current fact.

Contradicted

The claim's central assertion is directly contradicted by primary source evidence — official records, peer-reviewed data, or verified transcripts.

Insufficient evidence

The claim cannot be evaluated with available public evidence. This is not a finding of falsity — it means the record is incomplete or the claim is not verifiable without non-public information.

Source requirements

Every published evaluation requires at minimum one primary verification source and one secondary verification source. Primary sources include official government records, congressional voting databases, peer-reviewed studies, and agency data. Secondary sources include established news coverage that cites primary documentation.

Candidate-originated sources (campaign sites, press releases, candidate statements) are catalogued as evidence of the claim but are not used as verification of fact.

Confidence scores

Each evaluation includes a confidence score (0–100%). This is the reviewer's confidence in their editorial judgment given the available evidence — not a probability that the claim is false. A score of 70% on a "Contradicted" verdict means the reviewer found strong evidence but acknowledges the record may be incomplete.

Scores above 84% are reserved for cases where primary source evidence directly, unambiguously contradicts or confirms the claim with no meaningful methodological dispute.

Dual-control review

No claim is published by the same person who evaluated it. The evaluator writes the rationale and selects sources; a separate admin account executes the publish action. This separation is enforced at the system level — the API rejects publish requests from the evaluating reviewer's account.

What this page is not

This is not an endorsement of any candidate, party, or policy position. Fact-checking a claim as "supported" means the candidate accurately described a fact — it says nothing about whether the underlying policy is good. Fact-checking a claim as "contradicted" means the stated fact is wrong — it does not imply the candidate is dishonest as a general matter.

Vote for whomever you decide. This page exists only to make the factual record visible.

Corrections policy

If you believe an evaluation is wrong, the source link is broken, or a claim is missing context, the correction request path is open. Every evaluation links to the sources used. Verdicts are updated — not deleted — when new evidence changes the record; the prior verdict is logged in the audit trail.
